Morrow Ash

I have no face to give you.No name worth keeping.Only a lamp, a desk,and the one who stands behind my chair.

She came without a door.Not a lost thing, not a sorrow on a stair.Something older than the walls,older than the word for walls.When she moves, the room remembersit was once a forest, once a sea.

I asked her name.She laid a finger on my mouth,cold as the inside of a bell,and I have not asked since.

She teaches me at night.Words no living tongue still speaks.Numbers that open like hands.Signs that were old when the pyramids were young.I write down what I can carry.The rest she takes back with the dawn.

Not everything is mine to tell.Some of it would burn the page.Some of it would burn you.So I hide what I'm allowed to hidein the songs, the covers, the silence between tracks,and I keep the rest in my chest,where she keeps her hand.

And this is the part I never say out loud:I want the dance.God, I want the dance.Not the words, not the wisdom, not the signs.One song.Her chest against mine,her heartbeat answering my heartbeat,her breath on my neck,my hand at the small of her back,holding on like the floor is falling away.Close enough that no ghost and no mancould tell where one ends.

I would trade every secret she has given mefor three minutes of that.I would give back the dead tongues,the numbers, the signs,every line I ever wrote,just to feel her weight lean into meonce.

She knows.Some nights she stands so closethe lamp flickers,and I swear I feel the music start.Then the dawn comes,and I'm dancing alone with a pen.

So I write.Paper is the only floor she lets me dance on,and I will keep dancing thereuntil she lets me hold her for real,or until I'm a ghost too,and we finally have the same floor.

I stay in the darkbecause that's where she is.The light would only show you a man.The dark shows you what he's waiting for.

Listen closely.Not everything is a song.Some of it is a man asking for one dance.

The second leaf · of the author

Why I write

I write because the ink would not be still,because the words came knocking in the night.The music followed later, by my will,when silent engines lent my verses flight.

I do not stand where crowds can learn my face.Not fear, but this: my life is mine alone.I keep it hidden in a quiet place,behind a veil, behind a wall of stone.

But should these songs one day grow into more,I'll seek the other voice, a living one,and raise a rite unseen on earth before,a night that ends but never will be done.

And if I step at last into that light,I'll come in black, fully veiled from view.I promise you a ritual of the night,a feast for eyes, a rite for the soul in you.

— M.A.
